## Leadership (10)

**1. In a mastery (task-involving) motivational climate, success is mainly judged by…**  
A. Personal improvement, effort and learning  
B. Beating teammates in selection  
C. Match results and league position  
D. Praise from the head coach  
*Answer: A. A mastery climate defines success through improvement and effort. An ego (performance) climate defines it by comparison with others.*

**2. Self-determination theory says motivation is supported when three needs are met. Which set?**  
A. Autonomy, competence, relatedness  
B. Discipline, effort, reward  
C. Confidence, focus, control  
D. Challenge, support, feedback  
*Answer: A. Deci and Ryan: autonomy, competence and relatedness.*

**3. A team has high psychological safety when players…**  
A. Never hear critical feedback  
B. Are guaranteed playing time  
C. Feel able to speak up, ask questions and make mistakes without humiliation  
D. Avoid conflict with staff  
*Answer: C. Edmondson: a shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking. It sits alongside high standards, not instead of them.*

**4. Which feedback is most likely to change a player's next decision?**  
A. "Good job, keep going."  
B. "Be better on the ball."  
C. "That's not good enough at this level."  
D. "You received square-on, so your only option was back. Open your hips before the ball arrives."  
*Answer: D. Specific, timely feedback about the cue and the decision gives the player something to do next time.*

**5. Which is an autonomy-supportive coaching behaviour?**  
A. Using sprints as punishment for mistakes  
B. Offering choices within clear limits and explaining the reason for a rule  
C. Withholding information so players stay dependent  
D. Praising only the starters  
*Answer: B. Choice within structure, plus a rationale, supports autonomy without lowering standards.*

**6. A senior player openly challenges your tactical decision mid-session. The strongest first response is to…**  
A. Send them off the field immediately  
B. Acknowledge it briefly, keep the session moving, and talk it through privately afterwards  
C. Stop the session for a ten-minute debate  
D. Ignore it completely, now and later  
*Answer: B. Protect the session's flow and the player's dignity, then resolve it one-to-one. Ignoring it entirely lets the issue grow.*

**7. Why should all staff share the same game-model vocabulary?**  
A. It makes the coaching manual longer  
B. Players hear consistent cues from every coach, which speeds learning  
C. The Laws of the Game require it  
D. So players don't have to think  
*Answer: B. Shared language reduces mixed messages and lets every coach reinforce the same principles.*

**8. In Gibbs' reflective cycle, which stage comes straight after Analysis?**  
A. Conclusion  
B. Description  
C. Evaluation  
D. Feelings  
*Answer: A. Description, Feelings, Evaluation, Analysis, Conclusion, Action plan.*

**9. Best practice when telling a player they are not selected is to…**  
A. Tell them individually, give the reason, and name what to work on  
B. Post the team sheet and say nothing  
C. Let the captain tell them  
D. Wait until they ask  
*Answer: A. Direct, private, specific, with a path forward. It keeps trust intact.*

**10. A team talk with ten tactical points is most likely to…**  
A. Improve recall of each point  
B. Raise intensity  
C. Have no effect  
D. Reduce what players retain and act on  
*Answer: D. Working memory is limited. Two or three clear messages are retained and acted on far better.*

## Coaching training (10)

**1. In a constraints-led approach, shrinking the pitch is a change to which type of constraint?**  
A. Task  
B. Environmental  
C. Individual  
D. Organismic  
*Answer: A. Rules, pitch size, numbers and equipment are task constraints. Weather, surface and culture are environmental. Height, fitness and skill are individual.*

**2. Representative learning design means practice should…**  
A. Copy professional drills exactly  
B. Be the same every week  
C. Remove opponents so players can focus  
D. Keep the information players use in matches: opponents, teammates, direction  
*Answer: D. Practice should keep the perception-action links of the game so learning transfers to matches.*

**3. Making a possession area larger, with the same numbers, usually makes keeping the ball…**  
A. Easier  
B. Harder  
C. No different  
D. Impossible to measure  
*Answer: A. More space means more time on the ball and less pressure. Shrink the area to raise the challenge.*

**4. Compared with 11v11, small-sided games with fewer players per team typically give each player…**  
A. More touches and more involvement  
B. Fewer touches  
C. Less physical load  
D. No decisions  
*Answer: A. Fewer players means more ball contacts and more frequent decisions per player.*

**5. Guided discovery is when the coach…**  
A. Tells the player exactly what to do  
B. Asks questions that lead the player to find the solution  
C. Demonstrates and walks away  
D. Stays silent all session  
*Answer: B. Questions direct attention to the right cues, so the player builds the solution and owns it.*

**6. When is a stop-freeze most justified?**  
A. Every two minutes, as routine  
B. When the coach wants a break  
C. When a pattern keeps recurring and the whole group needs to see the picture  
D. Whenever a goal is scored  
*Answer: C. Use it sparingly, for a recurring problem that matters to the group. Keep it short: freeze, rewind, show, replay.*

**7. The main benefit of in-flow coaching is that it…**  
A. Gives the coach more talking time  
B. Corrects without stopping play, protecting intensity and repetitions  
C. Replaces planning  
D. Only works with professionals  
*Answer: B. A short cue during play keeps the session moving and players active.*

**8. The strongest reason for choosing a session topic is that it…**  
A. Appeared in a famous coach's book  
B. Is the coach's favourite drill  
C. Links to a game-model principle and a problem seen in the last match  
D. Needs no equipment  
*Answer: C. Assessors look for a clear thread: game model, then match observation, then session objective, then coaching.*

**9. In a typical tactical-periodisation week, which day usually uses larger spaces and longer efforts?**  
A. MD-1  
B. MD-2  
C. MD-3  
D. MD+1  
*Answer: C. A common morphocycle: MD-4 tension (small spaces), MD-3 duration (large spaces, longer efforts), MD-2 speed, MD-1 activation.*

**10. Whole-part-whole means…**  
A. Only train set pieces  
B. Run three different drills  
C. Play the game, isolate a part to practise, then return to the game  
D. Coach the group, then individuals, then the group  
*Answer: C. Start with the game to see the problem, practise the part, then put it back in the game to check it transfers.*

## Coaching games (10)

**1. A strong half-time talk usually…**  
A. Covers every mistake from the first half  
B. Changes the whole game plan every time  
C. Is mostly about refereeing decisions  
D. Lets players settle, then gives two or three clear points and one final message  
*Answer: D. Settle, prioritise, simplify. Players need a few actionable points to take back out.*

**2. A game plan should be built from…**  
A. Last season's tactics  
B. The opponent only  
C. Your game model, adapted to the opponent, available players and context  
D. Whatever the best player prefers  
*Answer: C. The game model is the constant. The game plan adjusts it for this opponent, on this day.*

**3. Which is a commonly used pressing trigger?**  
A. The opposing goalkeeper kicks long  
B. The opponent switches play perfectly  
C. A slow backward or square pass, or a poor first touch  
D. Our team is tired  
*Answer: C. Triggers signal a moment when the ball carrier is vulnerable: backward passes, poor touches, receivers facing their own goal, the ball going toward the touchline.*

**4. "Rest defence" refers to…**  
A. Players kept behind the ball while attacking, to stop counter-attacks  
B. Defending only when tired  
C. Recovery training after matches  
D. Defending deep for the whole match  
*Answer: A. Your structure behind the ball while in possession, ready for the moment you lose it.*

**5. A mid block means the team…**  
A. Presses man-to-man in the opponent's box  
B. Doesn't defend  
C. Sets its defensive block around the middle third and engages when the ball enters that zone  
D. Defends on the edge of its own box  
*Answer: C. The defensive line and the height of engagement sit in the middle third.*

**6. From the touchline, in-game instructions work best when they are…**  
A. Constant and detailed  
B. Short, specific, and timed for stoppages or nearby players  
C. Directed at the referee  
D. Given only by substitutes  
*Answer: B. Players under match load can't process long instructions. Use stoppages, the captain, and short pre-agreed cues.*

**7. After an emotional defeat, the best time for detailed feedback is usually…**  
A. Brief comments now, detailed video review at the next session  
B. Straight away, in full, in the dressing room  
C. Never  
D. A group text that evening  
*Answer: A. Emotion blocks learning. Keep it short after the match and go into detail when players can process it.*

**8. Leading by one goal with ten minutes left, which adjustment fits most game models?**  
A. Protect rest defence and manage risk in possession  
B. Drop all pressing triggers  
C. Send both full-backs forward  
D. Substitute the goalkeeper  
*Answer: A. Game-state management: limit exposure to counters while keeping the ball sensibly.*

**9. Counter-pressing (gegenpressing) is…**  
A. Pressing only from goal kicks  
B. Pressing immediately after losing the ball, to win it back quickly  
C. Dropping into a low block after losing the ball  
D. Man-marking the striker  
*Answer: B. An immediate reaction to losing the ball, while the opponent is still disorganised.*

**10. Scouting an opponent is most useful when you focus on…**  
A. Their kit colours  
B. Everything they do, equally  
C. The few things that most affect your plan, such as their build-up shape and main threat  
D. Their league position only  
*Answer: C. Prioritise what changes your decisions. Detail that doesn't change the plan is noise.*

B. 11–12

B. Age limits in competition

*Answer: D. Early-born players are often more physically mature and get selected more, which can hide talent born later in the year.*

**3. Bio-banding groups players by…**

C. Club  
D. Skill test results  
*Answer: B. It reduces maturity mismatches and gives early and late developers different challenges.*

## Performance analysis (10)

**1. Expected goals (xG) is…**  
A. The goals a team should score in a season  
B. Goals minus shots  
C. A measure of possession

*Answer: D. Each shot gets a probability from features such as distance, angle and type of assist.*

**2. Your principle is "progress through the lines". Which KPI fits best?**  
A. Total possession %  
B. Distance covered  
C. Corners won  
D. Completed line-breaking passes, or entries into the final third  
*Answer: D. A KPI should measure the principle directly. Possession alone doesn't show progression.*

**3. PPDA (passes allowed per defensive action). A lower value usually means…**  
A. More intense pressing  
B. Less intense pressing  
C. More possession  
D. More goals conceded  
*Answer: A. The opponent completes fewer passes before you make a defensive action in the chosen zone.*

**4. Field tilt describes…**  
A. A sloping pitch  
B. The height of the offside line  
C. Each team's share of final-third possession or passes  
D. Wind direction  
*Answer: C. It shows territorial dominance in the attacking third.*

**5. Before coding a match, the most important step is to…**  
A. Pick the chart colours  
B. Code as fast as possible  
C. Use only automatic data  
D. Write clear definitions for each event so coding is consistent  
*Answer: D. Operational definitions make data reliable between coders and across matches.*

**6. Which is a process measure rather than an outcome measure?**  
A. Goals scored  
B. Controlled entries into the box  
C. Final score  
D. Points won  
*Answer: B. Process measures reflect how you play and predict outcomes over time. Outcomes are noisy in single matches.*

**7. One match shows an unusual drop in pass completion. The best response is to…**  
A. Drop the midfield

C. Check the context and the trend over several matches first

**8. For a team video meeting, good clip selection means…**

B. A few clips linked to one or two principles, mixing what to repeat and what to fix  
C. Only goals conceded

*Answer: B. Focused, principle-linked and balanced clips are what players retain.*

**9. Inter-rater reliability checks whether…**  
A. Two analysts coding the same footage get the same results

D. The data is published

**10. Your data says you press well, but video shows players jogging after losing the ball. You should…**

## Laws & safeguarding (10)

**1. There is no offside offence when a player receives the ball directly from…**  
A. A free kick  
B. A throw-in, goal kick or corner kick  
C. A penalty rebound  
D. A teammate's pass in their own half  
*Answer: B. Law 11: no offside offence directly from a goal kick, throw-in or corner kick.*

**2. A player in an offside position is penalised only when they…**  
A. Are in the opponents' half  
B. Are near the goalkeeper  
C. Are behind the ball  
D. Become involved in active play  
*Answer: D. Being in an offside position is not an offence in itself. Involvement in active play is.*

**3. Where the competition allows, the Laws permit up to five substitutes, used in…**  
A. Unlimited stoppages  
B. Half-time only  
C. At most three opportunities, plus half-time  
D. Two opportunities  
*Answer: C. Law 3: five substitutes in no more than three opportunities during play; half-time doesn't count as one.*

**4. The U.S. Center for SafeSport's MAAPP requires one-on-one interactions with minor athletes to be…**  
A. Avoided with no exceptions  
B. Approved by the referee  
C. Observable and interruptible  
D. Recorded on video  
*Answer: C. The Minor Athlete Abuse Prevention Policies limit one-on-one interactions to observable and interruptible settings, with limited exceptions.*

**5. A player shows signs of a possible concussion. The correct action is to…**  
A. Remove them from play with no return that day, then follow a graduated return after medical clearance  
B. Check they know the score, then continue  
C. Let them finish the half  
D. Give them water and continue  
*Answer: A. When in doubt, sit them out. Return to play follows a step-by-step protocol after clearance.*

**6. Under U.S. Soccer's heading guidelines, players aged 10 and under…**  
A. May head freely  
B. May head only in training  
C. May head only in matches  
D. Should not head the ball  
*Answer: D. No heading for players 10 and under. Heading in training is limited for ages 11–13.*

**7. A defender denies an obvious goal-scoring opportunity with a foul in their own penalty area while genuinely trying to play the ball. The sanction is…**  
A. Penalty and red card  
B. Penalty and yellow card  
C. Indirect free kick only  
D. No sanction  
*Answer: B. Law 12: when the offence is an attempt to play the ball, the penalty comes with a caution, not a sending-off.*

**8. The goalkeeper handles a ball deliberately kicked to them by a teammate. The restart is…**  
A. Penalty kick  
B. Indirect free kick  
C. Direct free kick  
D. Dropped ball  
*Answer: B. An indirect free kick to the opponents.*

**9. A throw-in goes directly into the opponents' goal untouched. The result is…**  
A. Goal  
B. Goal kick  
C. Corner kick  
D. Retake  
*Answer: B. A goal can't be scored directly from a throw-in. If it enters the opponents' goal, it's a goal kick.*

**10. Can a goal be scored directly from a kick-off?**  
A. Yes, against the opponents  
B. No, never  
C. Only in extra time  
D. Only after a teammate touches it  
*Answer: A. Law 8: a goal may be scored directly against the opponents from a kick-off.*

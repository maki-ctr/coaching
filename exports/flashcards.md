## Leadership

- **Mastery climate**: An environment where success means improvement and effort, and mistakes are part of learning.
- **Self-determination theory**: Motivation thrives when autonomy, competence and relatedness are supported.
- **Psychological safety**: A shared belief that it's safe to speak up, ask and make mistakes. It works alongside high standards.
- **Gibbs' reflective cycle**: Description, Feelings, Evaluation, Analysis, Conclusion, Action plan.

## Coaching training

- **Rondo**: A possession game with an overload (e.g. 5v2) in a small area. Trains passing, body shape, scanning and pressing.
- **Constraints-led approach**: Shaping learning by changing task, environment or individual constraints so the desired behaviours emerge.
- **Representative learning design**: Practice that keeps the key information of the game: opponents, teammates, direction and real decisions.
- **Guided discovery**: Coaching through questions that lead players to find the solution themselves.
- **Stop-freeze**: Stopping play to show the whole group a picture. Use sparingly and keep it short: freeze, rewind, show, replay.
- **In-flow coaching**: Short cues during play, without stopping. Protects intensity and repetitions.
- **Whole-part-whole**: Game, then an isolated part, then back to the game to check transfer.
- **Morphocycle**: The weekly training structure built around match day in tactical periodisation (e.g. MD-4 tension, MD-3 duration, MD-2 speed, MD-1 activation).
- **MD-1**: The day before a match: short, sharp, low load. Set pieces and activation.

## Coaching games

- **Game model**: The team's agreed way of playing in every moment of the game, written as principles and sub-principles. It stays constant; game plans adapt it.
- **Game plan**: How the game model is adapted for one match: opponent, players available, conditions, game state.
- **Principle**: A behaviour the team wants to see in a moment of the game, e.g. 'find the free player between the lines'. Sub-principles break it into specific actions.
- **Moments of the game**: In possession, out of possession, transition to defend, transition to attack, plus set pieces.
- **Rest defence**: The players and shape kept behind the ball while attacking, ready to stop a counter-attack.
- **Counter-press**: Pressing immediately after losing the ball to win it back while the opponent is disorganised.
- **Pressing trigger**: A cue that tells the team to press: backward pass, poor touch, receiver facing own goal, ball to the touchline.
- **Block height**: Where the defensive block sets up: high press (opponent's third), mid block (middle third), low block (own third).
- **Half-space**: The vertical channel between the centre and the wing. Receiving there opens more passing angles and options to turn.
- **Third-man run**: Player A passes to B, who sets it for C running into space. C is the 'third man' the defence didn't track.
- **Overload to isolate**: Crowd one side to draw the opponent across, then switch to a 1v1 on the far side.
- **Line-breaking pass**: A pass that goes past one of the opponent's defensive lines to a teammate behind it.

## Player development

- **PHV**: Peak height velocity: the fastest point of the adolescent growth spurt. Around 13.5–14 for boys, earlier for girls.
- **Relative age effect**: Players born early in the selection year are over-represented in selected teams.
- **Bio-banding**: Grouping players by maturity status rather than chronological age.
- **Four-corner model**: A player profile across technical, tactical, physical and psychosocial.
- **SMART goal**: Specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound.

## Performance analysis

- **xG**: Expected goals: the probability that a shot is scored, based on historical shots with similar features.
- **PPDA**: Opponent passes allowed per defensive action in a chosen zone. Lower means more intense pressing.
- **Field tilt**: Each team's share of possession or passes in the final third.
- **KPI**: A measure tied directly to a game-model principle, used to judge whether the team plays the way it wants.
- **Process vs outcome**: Process measures (box entries, line breaks) show how you play. Outcomes (goals, points) are noisy in single matches.

## Laws & safeguarding

- **MAAPP**: SafeSport's Minor Athlete Abuse Prevention Policies, including keeping one-on-one interactions observable and interruptible.

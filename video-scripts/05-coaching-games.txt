Module 5. Coaching games.

Coaching on match day is a separate skill from coaching in training. The B course assesses it directly: the game plan, what the coach does during the game, the half-time talk, and the review afterwards.

It starts with the difference between a game model and a game plan. The game model is the team's constant way of playing. The game plan adapts it to one opponent, with the players available, on one day.

For Harbor FC U17 against Westside Academy, the plan had three key messages, and no more. Press their left centre-back, who is slow to turn. Switch play quickly, because they shift slowly to the far side. Protect the middle after we lose the ball. Westside's main threats were a quick striker who runs in behind, and long throws.

During the game, players under physical and emotional load cannot process long instructions from the touchline. Use stoppages, use the captain, and use short cues the team already knows. A one-word cue agreed in training works better than a paragraph shouted across the pitch.

Half-time is short, and it is easy to waste. A simple three-minute structure helps. In the first minute, let players settle. Water, breathing, and let the emotion drop, while the staff agree on what to say. From one minute to two and a half, give two or three points, specific, linked to the game plan, and aimed at the players they affect. For example: front three, press their left centre-back on the backward pass. Midfield, in the first two seconds after we lose it, attack the ball. In the last thirty seconds, give one message that every player takes back out. Against Westside, that message was: win it back in five seconds.

A common mistake is giving ten points at half-time. Players keep almost none of them. The app's half-time timer exists so Freddy can rehearse the talk out loud, within three minutes.

After the final whistle, keep it brief, especially after an emotional result. Emotion blocks learning. Do the detailed review at the next session, with video.

The review should produce three things. The numbers for the measures linked to the game model. What matched the game model. And the problem to train next week. After the Westside game, Harbor regained the ball within five seconds after only six of forty-one losses, fifteen percent, against a target of thirty percent. The build-up through the holding midfielder worked, and the overload on the left created the switch four times. The problem to train was the counter-press after losing the ball.

That problem became the next session topic, which closes the loop described in module three. In the app, one button turns the review into a new session plan, with the problem already filled in.

Collect evidence as you go. Each game plan with its review is a piece of evidence for the coaching games competency. Four of them, with filmed touchline coaching, make a strong portfolio.

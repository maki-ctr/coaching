Module 8. Assessment readiness.

The final weeks of the B course are about showing what you can do. That means two things: a portfolio of evidence, and answers that hold up when assessors ask questions.

Start with the portfolio. Aim for at least four strong pieces of evidence for each of the five competencies: leadership, coaching training sessions, coaching games, player development and performance analysis. Strong evidence comes from real coaching with a real team. Session plans with filmed sessions and reviews. Game plans with post-match reviews. Player plans with dated video reviews. Reports showing how analysis changed training. Reflections and feedback from course educators. Every piece should link back to the game model.

In the app, the portfolio shows progress for each competency, and the dashboard names the biggest gap and the next action. Work on the weakest competency first.

Now the questions. Assessors probe how a coach thinks: why you chose something, what evidence you have, and what you would change. A four-part shape keeps answers clear and under two minutes. Context, decision, evidence, and change.

Here is an example. The question is: tell us about a session that didn't work.

Context: U17s, week seven. The session was the counter-press, after we'd regained only fifteen percent of losses within five seconds against Westside.

Decision: I used a six against six game with three neutrals, and a point for regains inside five seconds, because I wanted the moment of loss to happen often.

Evidence: the video showed two point eight stoppages every ten minutes and only twelve percent questions. Intensity dropped every time I stopped play.

Change: I now keep stoppages to one per practice, coach in flow, and ask one question when I do stop. In the next match, we regained twenty-four percent.

That answer works because it uses real numbers, admits what went wrong, and ends with a change and its result.

Three mistakes to avoid. Opinions without evidence: name the number, the clip or the player's response. No change at the end: reflection means saying what you would do differently. And overlong answers: rehearse out loud, recorded on your phone, with a two-minute limit. The app's interview prep has twenty-five prompts across the five competencies, a two-minute timer and a self-score.

One point matters especially for a former international. Assessors may ask how a playing career shapes your coaching, and where it could limit you. Use the career as a resource: what you saw, what you learned from coaches who helped you. Then show how you coach players who don't think the way you did. Former professionals sometimes coach the player they were. Showing that you can coach the player in front of you is what assessors listen for.

Finally, use the course's own documents. When the official rubric and course guide arrive, they replace the placeholders in this app. Check every piece of evidence against the official criteria before the final assessments.

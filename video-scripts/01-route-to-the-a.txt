Module 1. The route to the A License.

This course follows one coach, Freddy Adu, on the U.S. Soccer pathway from the B License to the A License. Freddy is a former professional player and U.S. Men's National Team international. That background changes where he starts, but not what he has to prove.

Most coaches climb the pathway one license at a time. Former professional and national-team players can ask for a waiver. U.S. Soccer can waive the C License prerequisite for the B course based on professional or national-team playing experience. The waiver is not automatic. The hosting member decides, and the request goes through the U.S. Soccer Learning Center, following the Waiver Process FAQs. The criteria can change, so Freddy confirms them before he applies. He gathers his playing record: clubs, seasons and senior international caps, with supporting letters or federation confirmation.

Before the course starts, Freddy needs three things in place. His SafeSport training is current. His background screening is complete under the host's and the club's rules. And he has a team. The B course expects access to a U13 or older team playing eleven against eleven for the whole course. In this course, that team is Harbor FC U17.

The B course itself runs about twenty-four weeks in a blended format. There are weekly virtual classrooms, assignments, independent learning and two in-person meetings. Formats and dates vary by host and by year, so the course guide Freddy receives is the reference, not this video.

The B is assessed on what a coach does with a real team. It covers five competencies: leadership, coaching training sessions, coaching games, player development and performance analysis. Knowledge matters, but evidence of coaching matters more. That is why the study plan in this app builds evidence every week, starting with filmed sessions in week two.

After the B License is awarded, Freddy holds it for at least twelve months. The twelve months count from the award, not from the start of the course. During that year he keeps coaching in a performance environment, updates his game model from a full season, and organises his evidence by competency.

Then he chooses which A License to take. The A splits into A-Youth and A-Senior. A-Youth focuses on developing players for the professional game in a youth performance environment, roughly under-thirteen to under-nineteen. A-Senior focuses on leading a senior team in a high-performance environment. Both require the B License held for at least a year. For A-Senior, the coach also needs access to a high-performance senior team in a U.S.-based competition, or a college team.

The choice is not about preference. It depends on the team environment Freddy can coach in for the whole course. If he is coaching in an academy, A-Youth fits. If he has a route into a professional, semi-professional or college senior team, A-Senior fits.

Beyond the A sits the Pro License. It requires holding an A License for at least one year, and it is application-based.

So the route has a gate at every step: the waiver, the team, the course, the twelve months, and the team environment for the A. The rest of this course is about getting through the biggest gate, the B course, with evidence that holds up.

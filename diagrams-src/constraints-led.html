Constraints-led design

Instead of telling players the answer, change the practice so the behaviour you want happens more often. Three kinds of constraint to work with.

Module 3Coaching License Lab

Task

What the practice asks

Area size, numbers, rules, scoring, touch limits, equipment, time.

  • Bigger area: more time, easier
  • Fewer players: more touches each
  • Scoring rules reward the behaviour
Example

Shrink the area from 35×30 to 30×25 to raise the pressure on the ball.

Environment

The world around the practice

Surface, weather, light, crowd noise, team culture, expectations.

  • Match the next venue’s surface
  • Add noise or pressure for big games
  • Shape the culture around effort
Example

Train once on the smaller artificial pitch the opponents play on.

Individual

What each player brings

Height, maturity, fitness, skill, confidence, emotions.

  • Adjust roles, not standards
  • Use maturity, not just age
  • Watch confidence as well as skill
Example

A late-maturing full-back plays as a neutral to get more time on the ball.

Worked example

Want more counter-pressing? Change the task: award a point for winning the ball back within five seconds, and shrink the area so losses happen more often. Then watch what changes before you say anything.

Watch for
Pitch size is a task constraint, not an environmental one. A common quiz trap.
Changing several constraints at once, so you can’t tell which one made the difference.
Constraints don’t replace coaching. They create the moments; you still help players read them.
